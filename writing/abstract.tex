In most Robot Learning research, visuo-motor policies, world models, and world action models (WAMs) are conventionally trained once, offline, on a fixed dataset of
demonstrations, and then frozen at evaluation time. This approach provides no mechanism for models to correct
themselves from their own experience. However, a growing body of recent work 
\citep{wang2026adajepa,jiang2026robotttcontextscaling,ye2026dreamzero} suggests this need
not be the case.

This thesis investigates which methods, if any,
let a WAM be post or test-time trained on its own rollout data to improve task completion
performance on real-world robotic manipulation tasks, while avoiding catastrophic forgetting or loss of
plasticity \citep{elsayed2023utility}.
